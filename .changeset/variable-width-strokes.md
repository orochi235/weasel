A stroke can be drawn with a broad nib. `Stroke.nib` is `{ angle, ratio }`: the nib's edge in radians counterclockwise on screen, and its thinnest width as a fraction of its widest. The stroke is its full width moving across the nib and `ratio` of that moving along it, an elliptical pen's width in between, and it multiplies `vertexWidths` where both are set. Each side of a corner keeps its own width, so a square drawn with a level nib has thin level sides and full upright ones right up to the joins; a round join's arc runs between the two. `strokeToPath`, the renderer's ribbon cache, and SVG export follow it, and since a nib only ever narrows a stroke, bounds and culling are unchanged. `@weasel-js/stroke` adds `nibWidthScale`, the nib as a `WidthScale`, and `widestStrokeWidth`, which culling, picking, and flattening now share; picking used to ignore `vertexWidths`. Draw's stroke properties gain a Nib switch with its angle and thinnest ratio.

`useWidthTool` (Shift+W) edits `vertexWidths` on the canvas: a drag on an anchor sets its width to twice the pointer's distance from it, and a drag between two anchors first inserts one there with `insertAnchorOnSegment`, so the path keeps its shape. Either is previewed live and committed as one undo entry. Alt-click on an anchor clears its width, so it takes the stroke's `width` again. The stroke last touched shows its anchors, ringed where they have a width.

A dashed stroke now keeps its per-vertex widths, which it used to drop. The anchor-parameter rule that trimming and dashing share is `anchorParamsBetween` in `@weasel-js/geom/tessellate`.

SVG has no variable width. `serializeSvg` writes a stroke with `vertexWidths` or a `nib` as its outline: the path keeps its stroke attributes at `stroke-width="0"`, which draws nothing, with the real width, the per-vertex widths, the nib, and its markers in the `wzl` namespace, and the outline follows as a filled path in the stroke's paint that draws the marker heads. A node with an opacity wraps both in one group carrying it, so they composite as one layer. Other viewers draw the outline; `parseSvg` drops it and reads the stroke back off the path, and a transform it bakes in scales the per-vertex widths with the width.
