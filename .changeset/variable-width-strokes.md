A stroke can be drawn with a broad nib. `Stroke.nib` is `{ angle, ratio }`: the nib's edge in radians counterclockwise on screen, and its thinnest width as a fraction of its widest. The stroke is its full width moving across the nib and `ratio` of that moving along it, an elliptical pen's width in between, and it multiplies `vertexWidths` where both are set. Each side of a corner keeps its own width, so a square drawn with a level nib has thin level sides and full upright ones right up to the joins; a round join's arc runs between the two. `strokeToPath`, the renderer's ribbon cache, and SVG export follow it, and since a nib only ever narrows a stroke, bounds and culling are unchanged. `@weasel-js/stroke` adds `nibWidthScale`, the nib as a `WidthScale`, and `widestStrokeWidth`, which culling, picking, and flattening now share; picking used to ignore `vertexWidths`. Draw's stroke properties gain a Nib switch with its angle and thinnest ratio.

A `vertexWidths` entry is a `VertexWidth`: a number, the full width centered on the path as before, or a `{ left, right }` pair giving each edge its own distance from the path, `right` being the side the normal `(−dy, dx)` points to, below a path running in +x on screen. Every reader resolves an entry through `vertexHalfWidths(entry, fallback)` in `@weasel-js/paint`: the tessellator, which carries the difference between the sides as the new `Polyline.offsets` through trimming, dashing, and the nib, and centers caps between the two edges; `strokeToPath`; the inner and outer ribbon; bounds, culling, and picking, through `widestHalfWidth`; and SVG, which writes a pair as `left/right` in `wzl:vertex-widths`. `vertexWidthFromSides` writes equal sides back as a number, and `scaleVertexWidth` scales either form. An entry of zero is now a zero width; it used to take the stroke's `width`.

`useWidthTool` (Shift+W), in `@weasel-js/path-tools`, edits `vertexWidths` by dragging the stroke's edges. Hovering near either edge marks the grab point on it and the width point a press there moves: the anchor at that spot, or one inserted between two with `insertAnchorOnSegment`, so the path keeps its shape. The grabbed edge follows the pointer along the path's normal and the other mirrors it, writing a number; dragging inward pinches the stroke, down to zero. Alt-drag moves the grabbed edge alone and writes a pair. The drag moves the edge as drawn, so under a nib the stored width changes by more. Every drag is previewed live and committed as one undo entry. Alt-click on a width point clears it, so it takes the stroke's `width` again. The stroke last touched shows its anchors, with a line across the stroke at each that has a width. `strokeEdgeNear` in `@weasel-js/stroke` is the hit test behind it: the edge of a stroke nearest a point, measured along the path's normal, with each side's distance as drawn.

A dashed stroke now keeps its per-vertex widths, which it used to drop. The anchor-parameter rule that trimming and dashing share is `anchorParamsBetween` in `@weasel-js/geom/tessellate`.

SVG has no variable width. `serializeSvg` writes a stroke with `vertexWidths` or a `nib` as its outline: the path keeps its stroke attributes at `stroke-width="0"`, which draws nothing, with the real width, the per-vertex widths, the nib, and its markers in the `wzl` namespace, and the outline follows as a filled path in the stroke's paint that draws the marker heads. A node with an opacity wraps both in one group carrying it, so they composite as one layer. Other viewers draw the outline; `parseSvg` drops it and reads the stroke back off the path, and a transform it bakes in scales the per-vertex widths with the width.
